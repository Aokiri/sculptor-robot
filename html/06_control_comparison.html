06 · How much of the pawn survives joint noise?¶

The Panda has 1,595 moves to carve a 42 cm chess pawn. Can feedback protect the 208 statue cubes while clearing the 1,607 marble cubes around them? We compare open and closed loop, each with the posture task on and off. Every run follows the same clock.

At 0.001 rad per step, closed loop with posture on preserved the whole statue and left 11 marble cubes in each of five runs. Replaying the corresponding commands open loop cost an average of 18.8 statue cubes and left 907.2 marble cubes.

The plots are computed from the 60 run summaries and the seed-0 arrival errors.

1. From joint command to contact¶

The STL, 3 cm voxels, initial Panda configuration, IK seed 0, target order and table PID come from notebook 05. Its noise-free, posture-on controller supplies the duration of every cut, withdrawal, joint transfer and approach. All four conditions therefore get the same time to reach each target, for 3,745.8 s of simulated time in total.

  • Closed loop. At each active step, RRMC recomputes the joint velocity from the actual joint angles and tip pose. Joint-space transfers use proportional feedback on the remaining joint error.
  • Open loop. For each posture setting, a noise-free run records those joint velocities before the joint stops are applied. The arm replays the velocities from its actual position, including any accumulated error.
  • Posture. The primary gain is 3 in every run. With posture on, the existing null-space term pulls towards the IK reference with gain 2. With posture off, its gain is zero.

For an active sample of duration $\Delta t=0.05$ s, the simulator applies $q_{k+1}=\mathrm{clip}(q_k+\dot q_k\Delta t+w_k,\,q_{\min},q_{\max})$. Each of the seven components of $w_k$ is an independent normal joint displacement with standard deviation $\sigma$ radians per step. A common seed gives all four conditions the same sequence of displacements. The table turns under its PID while the arm holds the last joint configuration.

At every sample, including setup, forward kinematics gives the tip position. The actual table angle maps it into the block frame. The first time the point lies inside a present cube, the cube is removed. This includes the statue, incidental marble and contact during withdrawal or table rotation. A missed target stays until a later contact. A tip can cross material between two samples without being observed. The result measures point contact at 50 ms intervals. Chisel force and collisions of the body or links would require separate measurements.

The integration, per-step noise and end-of-move measurement follow Antonio Paolillo's SUPSI exercise move_left_right in 6_RobotControl.ipynb. Joint-velocity replay is the variant suggested in issue 7. The project implements RRMC and its null-space term.

2. What counts as an arrival?¶

The tool begins at the first cube. We report that setup point separately. For each subsequent approach or cut, attempt_end_step records the last allotted step, whether or not the tip reaches the cube. Arrival error is the Euclidean tip-to-centre distance at that step. The arrival RMSE gives all 1,595 moves equal weight. The 1 mm count uses the same endpoints. Removal can happen sooner because the voxel extends 15 mm from its centre on each axis.

Notebook 05 also logged the distance on all samples with a target, including withdrawals. In its saved log, 358 of the 1,596 target IDs last appear during a withdrawal. Their median last-sample error is about 89 mm. The per-run table retains this historical all-sample RMSE in metres as tip_rmse_m.

The campaign uses $\sigma=0$, 0.001 and 0.005 rad per active step and seeds 0 through 4. After the zero-noise check, twelve pilot runs covered all four conditions at 0.001 rad and seeds 0 through 2. Each pilot counts once in the final 60. At zero noise, the five seeds test repeatability.

def plot_seeds(metrics: list[tuple[str, str]], title: str) -> None:
    """Show five individual seeds and their mean and sample SD for each condition."""
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(len(metrics), len(noises), figsize=(11.6, 6.5), layout="constrained")
    for row, (field, unit) in enumerate(metrics):
        for column, noise in enumerate(noises):
            ax = axes[row, column]
            for x, label in enumerate(conditions):
                selected = (done.noise_std == noise) & (done["control / posture"] == label)
                group = done.loc[selected].sort_values("seed")
                values = group[field].to_numpy()
                offsets = (group.seed.to_numpy() - 2) * 0.065
                ax.scatter(x + offsets, values, s=22, color=colors[x], alpha=0.72, zorder=3)
                ax.errorbar(x, values.mean(), yerr=values.std(ddof=1), fmt="D", markersize=4,
                            color="#25262b", linewidth=1.3, capsize=4, zorder=4)
            ax.set_xticks(range(4), ticks)
            ax.set_xlim(-0.45, 3.45)
            ax.set_axisbelow(True)
            ax.grid(axis="y", color="#dedede", linewidth=0.7)
            ax.spines[["top", "right"]].set_visible(False)
            if column == 0:
                ax.set_ylabel(unit)
            if row == 0:
                ax.set_title(f"Noise {noise:g} rad/step")
            if field == "arrival_rmse_mm":
                ax.set_yscale("log")
            else:
                ax.yaxis.set_major_locator(MaxNLocator(nbins=5, integer=True))
                low, high = ax.get_ylim()
                low, high = min(0, low), max(1, high)
                pad = 0.04 * (high - low)
                ax.set_ylim(low - pad, high + pad)
        if field == "arrival_rmse_mm":
            low, high = axes[row, 0].get_ylim()
            for ax in axes[row, 1:]:
                left, right = ax.get_ylim()
                low, high = min(low, left), max(high, right)
            for ax in axes[row]:
                ax.set_ylim(low / 1.2, high * 1.12)
    fig.suptitle(title)
    plt.show()

Each dot is one seed, ordered from 0 to 4 within a condition. A diamond marks the mean and the bar extends one sample standard deviation on either side ($n=5$, denominator $n-1$). The vertical scales adjust to the observations in each panel. At 0.005 rad with posture on, open loop removed fewer statue cubes on average than closed loop (12.2 vs 18.0), while leaving 1,457.2 marble cubes against 6.0. With this much noise, the smaller damage count came with very little carving. At zero noise, the new first-contact rule removed the same cubes as the saved 05 run. Of the 1,596 removals, 1,595 happened earlier, including two first contacts while another cube was the target.

plot_seeds([("arrival_rmse_mm", "Arrival RMSE [mm], log scale"),
            ("within_1mm", "Arrivals within 1 mm")], "How close was the tip when each movement ended?")

At zero noise with posture on, all 1,595 moves finished within 1 mm. At 0.001 rad, closed loop with posture on still preserved the statue, although only 169.4 arrivals per run met 1 mm on average. A 3 cm voxel can be carved well away from its centre. Switching posture off increased the mean closed-loop arrival RMSE from 2.58 to 3.70 mm. At 0.005 rad the corresponding difference was smaller (12.15 and 12.61 mm), and both settings damaged the statue. The RMSE panels use a common logarithmic scale.

With posture on, the open-loop median rose from 40.27 mm over the first 100 moves to 163.35 mm over the last 100. The corresponding closed-loop medians were 2.53 and 2.11 mm. Open-loop error also falls over parts of the route. With feedback and posture off, six arrivals exceeded 10 mm, with a peak of 83.11 mm at move 720. Enabling posture kept all 1,595 arrivals below 10 mm for this seed.

The comparisons cover one STL and five paired disturbance sequences per nonzero noise level. They answer what the tip removes in the nominal time budget. A controller allowed extra settling time would answer a different question.

Reproduce the figures¶

Run all cells from a fresh Python kernel. The two CSVs above are sufficient. To regenerate the trajectories and tables, run uv run python -m experiments.control_comparison --jobs 4 from the repository root. The experiment saves one local NPZ per run.
